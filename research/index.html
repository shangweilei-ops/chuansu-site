Multi-Modal Deep Learning for Alzheimer's Drug Repositioning

ICICR 2026 · Paris, France · June 12–14, 2026 · Paper ID: A20260302530339

Authors: Xiuqin Shang, Chuansu Shang  ·  Conference: 2026 3rd International Conference on Intelligent Computing and Robotics

A multi-modal deep learning framework that integrates Graph Neural Networks, Transformers, and Knowledge Graphs with explicit uncertainty quantification — applied to drug repositioning for Alzheimer's disease. The work combines heterogeneous biomedical signals (chemical structure, protein interaction, prior knowledge) to surface candidates that single-modality models miss, and quantifies how confident the model should be in each prediction.

Why this is on a materials science site: the same question runs through all my work — how does a fragile system (a building, a wound, a brain) get protected before failure? Drug repositioning is the same problem at another scale. This is my first cross-disciplinary publication.

Why hydrogel?

Working on fire-suppressant materials taught me the most important thing wasn't "which material can extinguish" — it was why this material still works under extreme conditions. Hydrogel is a natural extension: the same molecular-network question, but now facing the body instead of the environment.

A hydrogel that maintains structural integrity under flame conditions can often do the same in a moist wound environment. A molecular design that blocks heat conduction can sometimes be translated into a physical barrier against cytokine diffusion. That feeling of "different scenes, same material doing the same job" is the core drive behind this work.

Current threads

  • Material structure: crosslink density, pore distribution, water content vs. mechanical behavior
  • Biocompatibility: coupling with the skin / wound microenvironment, support for cell migration
  • Application scenarios: large-area burn coverage and chronic wound dressings

From fire to skin — the through-line

I've stopped writing fire-suppression and skin-repair as two separate projects. They're the two ends of the same project: protecting fragile systems. Fire-retardant coating protects the building and the human body from thermal runaway; hydrogel protects healing tissue from secondary damage.

"Hydrogel isn't a new activity — it's the same material thinking, expanded into a different scale."
