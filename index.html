01 · Thesis

A single question, asked three ways.

Fire extinguishing and skin repair look like very different problems — one faces the environment, one faces the body. Both answer the same underlying question: how do materials protect fragile systems before failure becomes visible?

I started in ninth grade with fire-suppressant materials and brought a prototype into real homes and schools. Then I built Chemisfun, translating complex chemistry for ordinary readers. Then I walked into Prof. Garcia's lab to study hydrogels, pushing the same material thinking from environment into the body — wound healing, tissue repair, skin barrier.

One thread ties it together: using materials to protect fragile systems.

02 · Architecture

Three layers, one conviction.

Material layer. Product layer. Human layer. The third is where I stand apart from most research-oriented applicants — real safety lives in whether a person is willing to believe the risk.

LAYER 01

Material

What materials can extinguish, cool, and repair?

From hydrogel networks to fire-retardant coatings to bio-compatible scaffolds — I want to understand why materials work in extreme conditions, not just whether they work.

  • Hydrogel networks
  • Fire-retardant coatings
  • Bio-compatible scaffolds
LAYER 02

Product

How do materials reach real homes, schools, dorms, communities?

Something that "works" in a lab isn't enough for me. My fire-suppressant prototypes have been deployed into campus dorms and community fire-safety stations. From beaker to shelf — every millimeter has to be earned.

  • SafeHome · fire-safety kits
  • School & dorm pilots
  • Community deployment
LAYER 03

Human

Why do people know the risk, yet not believe disaster can happen?

I research fire safety, run science communication, know the danger. But the moment the fire alarm rang, my first reaction was "this can't be real". That one second taught me — the real gap is not knowing, but believing.

  • Risk perception
  • Behavioral design
  • Science communication
"The biggest gap in fire safety isn't knowing — it's believing. My work is to close that gap, one material, one product, one honest sentence at a time." — From my Common App essay
