Thesis
Close the knowing / believing gap

Why I started it

While researching fire suppression, I realized quickly: the biggest bottleneck isn't the material, isn't manufacturing — it's cognition. Ordinary people don't buy a suppression patch not because it's expensive, but because they don't believe they'll need it.

Chemisfun started from me wanting to solve this problem: make chemistry a story people can believe.

What I write about

  • Everyday chemistry myths (is "sugar-free" really safe for teeth?)
  • The real stories behind material science (how a fire-retardant coating actually works)
  • Risk and safety (why lithium batteries self-ignite)
  • The chemistry of skin, wounds, and health

How I write — the rules I keep

"Don't cut the jargon, don't dumb it down, don't exaggerate. Be the honest friend."
  • Don't cut the jargon — explain the term clearly, don't dodge around it.
  • Don't dumb it down — readers are smarter than I think.
  • Don't exaggerate — danger has been exaggerated too many times; the cost of believing has been burned too many times.
  • Structure first — every piece must answer one specific question.

What this is for — intellectually

The kind of intellectual vitality Stanford values isn't "I learned knowledge" — it's reorganizing knowledge, transmitting it, and changing how other people understand it. Chemisfun is the vehicle for that.

The same applies to Penn and JHU: Penn looks for doers — product + impact + community; JHU looks for cross-disciplinary depth. Chemisfun isn't an "extra activity" — it's the human layer of the same thesis.
